# Music for the Telarchy snake videos: what works in the venue

Date: 2026-09-17. Method: the `steal-what-works` skill (measure the venue, base rate first, segment, check the confound, write the falsifier).
Question: what background music should a captions-only video use when it shows a market playing Snake inside a trading-terminal dashboard (2.5 minute videos and 45 second Shorts)?

## What is measured and what is not

Measured:
- View counts, durations, channels and descriptions for 355 unique YouTube search results (10 queries x 40 results, pulled with yt-dlp on 2026-09-17), and a second sample of 400 rows for Shorts, marble races, bar chart races and Google Snake bots.
- Full descriptions of 142 of those videos, scanned for music credits.
- Tempo, percussive share of energy, and loudness by quarter for 63 candidate tracks, computed with librosa from the streamed audio. The tempo estimate was checked against the BPM Kevin MacLeod publishes for his own tracks and agreed on all 9 (128 vs 129, 117 vs 117, 106 vs 108, 122 vs 123, 108 vs 108, 124 vs 123, 120 vs 123, 120 vs 117, 124 vs 123).
- Licence of every recommended track, read from the track's own page.

Not measured:
- I could not listen to anything. Genre and mood of candidate tracks come from the page tags and the numbers, not from ears. Somebody has to listen to the shortlist before one is picked.
- I could not analyse the audio of the YouTube videos themselves. After the metadata pull YouTube rate-limited this IP (HTTP 429, then "sign in to confirm you are not a bot"). The only way past it was to pass Viktor's logged-in browser cookies to the scraper, which I did not do. So "how the music is structured against the video" is known only for videos whose credits or the composer's own notes say so. That part of the pattern is inferred, and it is marked as such below.
- YouTube exposed no "Music in this video" field for any of the 142 videos (yt-dlp `track` and `artist` were empty on every one), so an uncredited video stays "undetermined".

## Base rate

355 unique results across the 10 queries: median 23,719 views, p90 3.87 million, p10 77 views. The distribution is as long-tailed as the skill warns. A typical "AI plays snake" upload from a small channel gets under 100 views (the bottom 40 relevant results range from 0 to 88).

## The pattern

1. **Two things win and they are different.** Among no-voiceover algorithm videos, the four biggest (28.7M, 13.7M, 6.6M, 5.6M) use no music at all: the data makes the sound, one tone per operation, pitched by the value. Among no-voiceover game-AI videos with a credit, the winners use a modern instrumental electronic bed with a real beat: TF2Llama (13.2M, Wintergatan and Neffex), Code Bullet's two captions-only snake videos (3.3M Audionautix, 1.35M Kevin MacLeod "EDM Detection Mode"), Viktor Bohush (1.06M, Silent Partner "Drop It").
2. **Genre and tempo.** Every credited winner is instrumental, has drums or a drum machine, and the two whose tempo is published sit at 90 BPM (grapes) and 128 BPM (EDM Detection Mode). The tempo of the other credited tracks is from my general knowledge of them (mid tempo), not measured. None is 8-bit. The only chiptune credit in the whole sample outside a Pokemon context is on a 67-view video, alongside a track tagged "Bouncy, Bright, Uplifting". That is one data point and it matches the owner's reaction to "Jump!": bouncy and cute reads as a toy, driving and neutral reads as a tool.
3. **Structure (partly inferred).** What the credits show: short videos use one track as a constant bed (Bohush, AlphaPhoenix, CB's first snake video). Videos with sections switch tracks at the section boundary (CB Snake Fusion: two tracks; TF2Llama: four tracks in 80 seconds, one per hack). MacLeod's own note on the track CB picked says it has "a significant change in theme at around 2 minutes" and another at 3:30, so the chosen beds are ones that evolve, not flat loops. I could not measure whether a drop is timed to the payoff in any of these videos, because the audio was not reachable. Treat "put the lift on the payoff" as an inference from the track choices, not a finding.
4. **Shorts.** The two relevant Shorts-length hits I could see (A* on a real map, 3.0M in 57 s; Hoever's 84 s perfect game, 3.8M) have no music credit, so nothing is measured for Shorts specifically. The sample also contained almost no true Shorts, because YouTube search does not return them well without a login.

### Confound check

Views here are mostly channel and age. Code Bullet's numbers are Code Bullet; Timo Bingmann's video is 13 years old. The sample cannot separate the music from the channel, and within-channel comparison is not possible because the music is unknown for most of each channel's videos. What survives the confound is narrower: (a) no top performer in the venue uses chiptune or a bouncy bright bed for this kind of content, and (b) the single most reliable audio pattern in algorithm visualisation is sound generated by the data. Both are about what the venue's audience already accepts, not proof that music moves views.

### What to copy

- A driving, neutral, instrumental electronic bed, 110 to 128 BPM, with drums, no vocals, no 8-bit leads. Modern or 80s-synth production both appear; bright and bouncy does not.
- For the 2.5 minute video: one track that evolves, or two tracks with the switch on the video's turning point (for example when the snake gets long and the book gets tight). Pick the cut so the track's loudest section sits over the end game.
- For the 45 second Short: start on the beat, no slow intro. Cut in at the point where the track is already at full level.
- Cheap and already proven in this exact venue: layer data-driven ticks over the bed (one soft tone per move or per trade, pitched by price). The top four no-voiceover algorithm videos are nothing but that. It also suits a terminal look better than any music choice. This is a product suggestion, not something I tested.

## Recommendation

Full video: "Drama" or "Back In The 80s" (CC0, zero paperwork), chosen by ear. Short: "Strange Signal" cut onto its drop, or the loud section of the same track as the full video so the channel sounds like one thing. If none of the CC0 tracks survive a listen, use "EDM Detection Mode" with the credit line and expect to dispute one claim. Whatever the bed, add soft data-driven ticks per move or trade; it is the one audio pattern the venue has rewarded at the 10M-plus level without any music at all.

## Falsifier

Base rate for this kind of upload from a small channel is a median under 100 views in the weak half and 23.7k across the whole search sample, so raw views will not tell us whether the music worked. The test that can fail: average view duration. If the next two full videos with the new bed do not beat the chiptune version's average percentage viewed (same length, same title pattern) by at least 5 points, the music was not the problem, and the next thing to change is the first 5 seconds, not the track.

## Dead ends

- yt-dlp `track`/`artist` fields: empty for all 142 videos.
- Web search for the music in Hoever's, OpenAI's and "ones and zeros" videos: nothing reliable.
- YouTube audio analysis: blocked by rate limiting; not bypassed with account cookies.
- Pixabay: not checked; its licence is not CC and its Content ID status varies per track.
