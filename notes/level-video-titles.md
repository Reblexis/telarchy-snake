# Titles and descriptions for the snake videos (2026-09-18)

Method: the steal-what-works skill. Sample: 722 YouTube search results over 20 queries
(AI learns to play snake, Twitch plays, sorting and pathfinding visualisations, snake
Shorts), pulled 2026-09-17 for the music survey (`notes/level-video-music.md`), titles
and view counts only. Medians, never means.

## Base rate

| | n | median views | p90 | under 1,000 views |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| long videos | 580 | 6,097 | 2,979,214 | 38% |
| Shorts | 142 | 558 | 99,745 | 56% |

These are mostly established channels. A new channel should expect well under the median.

## What the titles of the winners share (measured, long videos on topic, n=361)

| title has | yes (median) | no (median) |
|---|---|---|
| a number | 17,923 (n=106) | 3,994 |
| 45 characters or fewer | 11,724 (n=175) | 6,589 |
| first person (I / My / We) | 124,073 (n=21) | 6,673 |
| a superlative (perfect, impossible, best, insane) | 1,012 (n=59) | 14,183 |
| an ALL-CAPS word | 9,745 | 7,155 |
| explainer words (tutorial, python, explained) | 11,248, but p90 603,661 vs 3,140,096 | 6,843 |

Shorts (n=91): a number helps even more (7,494 vs 270); LONGER keyworded titles do better
(937 vs 169 for 45 characters or fewer), the opposite of long videos.

The grammar of the top of the list is "<who> Plays/Learns <game>": Twitch Plays Pokemon
(22.3M), A.I. Learns to play Flappy Bird (14.8M), OpenAI Plays Hide and Seek (11.0M),
Neural Network Learns to Play Snake (4.6M). The subject is the novelty, the game is
familiar, nothing else is in the title.

Confound: first person is 21 videos, mostly Code Bullet, DougDoug and EYstreem, so it
measures who posts, not the wording; treat it as unproven. The superlative penalty is
probably the same effect in reverse (small channels hype). The number and length effects
hold across many channels and are the ones to trust.
